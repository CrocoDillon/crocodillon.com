<p className="intro">I’d been meaning to write about em dashes for a while. Then I watched Theo Browne get to Matt Pocock’s Grill Me skill on YouTube and stop to count the dashes. Nine, by his count. Judging by the reaction, you’d think the skill had broken something.</p>

He tried the skill anyway.[^video] I kept thinking about the dashes.

I’ve always liked getting the little things right when I write. [Smart Quotes for Smart People](https://smartquotesforsmartpeople.com/) got me into curly quotes and apostrophes. I also kept [Wikipedia’s dash page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dash) handy when I needed to decide which dash to use. A hyphen joins the words in “cross-browser,” an en dash marks a range like “200–500,” and an em dash lets me interrupt myself mid-sentence—which comes in handy.

I was already using em dashes on this blog in 2014. Here’s one from back then and a couple from more recent posts.

- [Always catch LocalStorage security and quota exceeded errors](/blog/always-catch-localstorage-security-and-quota-exceeded-errors) (2014) gives Internet Explorer 9 a little credit by putting “like a good browser” between dashes.
- [Getting back to writing](/blog/getting-back-to-writing) (2026) has one at the moment I gave up on the old blog setup. “Eventually I lost the database credentials—and that was that.”
- [How I created a single element Slack loader](/blog/how-i-created-a-single-element-slack-loader) (2026) puts “not so humble” between dashes when I mention a title my colleagues gave me.

Now that LLMs have picked up the habit too, a dash I put there on purpose can make a sentence look machine-written. Apparently I’m supposed to tell my writing agent to make a worse punctuation choice so it’ll sound more like me. Awkward.

Still, I get where the advice comes from. Any stylistic device gets tiresome with repetition. If every other sentence has an aside, I start to lose track of what the paragraph was about. Sometimes a comma will do. Sometimes the aside is the interesting part and deserves a sentence of its own.

Whenever I install a skill that bans em dashes outright, I change the rule to something like this.

> Use em dashes sparingly. Keep them where the break helps the sentence read the way you meant it to.
