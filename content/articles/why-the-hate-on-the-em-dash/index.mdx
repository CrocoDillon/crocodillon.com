<p className="intro">I’ve been thinking about em dashes for a while. Then I watched a video where the host got to Matt Pocock’s Grill Me skill and stopped to count the dashes. Suddenly the skill was being judged by how many em dashes it had.</p>

The host tried the skill anyway. I kept thinking about the dashes.

I’ve always liked getting the little things right when I write. [Smart Quotes for Smart People](https://smartquotesforsmartpeople.com/) got me into curly quotes and apostrophes. I also kept [Wikipedia’s dash page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dash) handy when I needed to decide which dash to use. A hyphen joins the words in “cross-browser,” an en dash marks a range like “200–500,” and an em dash lets me interrupt myself mid-sentence—which comes in handy.

I was already using em dashes on this blog in 2014, back when I was writing about [LocalStorage security and quota errors](/blog/always-catch-localstorage-security-and-quota-exceeded-errors).

Now that LLMs have picked up the habit too, a dash I put there on purpose can make a sentence look machine-written. Apparently I’m supposed to tell my writing agent to make a worse punctuation choice so it’ll sound dumb, like humans. Awkward.

Still, I get where the advice comes from. Any stylistic device gets tiresome with repetition. If every other sentence has an aside, I start to lose track of what the paragraph was about. Sometimes a comma will do. Sometimes the aside is the interesting part and deserves a sentence of its own.

If an em dash makes the sentence easier to read, it’s earned its place. Deleting it on sight is the lazy edit.
